# Voiceover for the recorded native walkthrough

This script matches the **77-second [silent native walkthrough](../artifacts/submission/unpause-native-walkthrough.mp4)**. Read it warmly at about 128 words per minute, with a small breath between sections. The section times are editing cues, not spoken text. Record in a quiet room with the microphone a handspan from your mouth; leave two seconds of silence before and after the take.

The recording is actual Android emulator interaction with clearly labeled illustrative sample projects. It demonstrates the local return loop. For the final public hackathon entry, use the [full submission script](submission.md) and capture the configured, tested store purchase flow; this preview clip does not demonstrate RevenueCat checkout.

## 0:00–0:05 · The next step

This is Unpause, a way back into the things you love making.

## 0:05–0:17 · Begin a session

When you finally have ten spare minutes, remembering where you stopped can use most of them. Unpause keeps your next small step ready.

## 0:17–0:33 · Leave a checkpoint

After a little making, leave yourself a handoff: what changed, what to do next, how long it might take, and where you put the pieces. Your next session starts with context.

## 0:33–0:40 · Save your place

Add a photo if it helps, then save your place. The record stays on your device.

## 0:40–0:54 · Moments

Each return becomes a checkpoint. You can see the small steps adding up, without a streak to protect or a deadline to miss. Your work is still here when you are.

## 0:54–1:00 · Your day

Choose the time and energy you have today, and find something that fits.

## 1:00–1:17 · The project shelf

Across sewing, woodwork, art, or your own kind of making, this is one home for your half-made things. I’m Shivam Gupta, creator of Unpause. Less remembering. More making.

## Editing handoff

Add your recording as the audio track over the MP4. Import the prepared [English captions](../artifacts/submission/unpause-voiceover-captions.en.srt), then adjust their timing to your actual take. Their timestamps are a guide until your voice is recorded. Adjust pauses to the screen changes; do not speed up your voice to fit a mismatched take. The video has no existing audio to preserve.

Keep the sample label visible and avoid adding an approved-store badge or purchase-success overlay. The pitch deck and brief include the proposed business model; add those claims to the final demo only with accurate wording and any required live evidence. No music is needed for this calm product demonstration.
