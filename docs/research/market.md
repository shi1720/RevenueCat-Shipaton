# Unpause: market and product decision

Research checked 16 September 2026. Prepared for Shivam Gupta's hackathon entry. This is desk research, not completed customer validation. Prices are public reference points, not evidence that Unpause users will pay.

## Decision

Proceed with **Unpause**, a pocket handoff for unfinished physical projects. Its strongest promise is: **“Leave yourself a way back.”** A photo, the exact next action, and where the materials live make a project easier to resume when a small pocket of time opens up.

The consumer problem is relatable and visually demonstrable. The defensible product direction is the pause/resume ritual, especially time-matched re-entry, rather than a broad craft inventory. There are direct competitors. We must not pitch this as the first project-memory app, an uncontested category, or a proven business.

## Evidence of the problem

First-person craft discussions show recurring unfinished projects and homemade ways to preserve context. In a June 2026 sewing discussion, one participant explains that accumulating projects causes them to forget construction plans and lose their place. Another describes putting next steps on sticky notes before storing a project. That is strong qualitative evidence for the proposed handoff behavior, but it does not establish prevalence or willingness to pay. [Sewing discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/sewing/comments/1u4dxvf/who_fizzles_out_before_finishing_a_sewing_project/)

A separate discussion includes a maker who stores an abandoned project with a note to their future self, then returns months or years later. Several participants distinguish temporary frustration from a project they no longer want. Unpause should support both returning and consciously letting go; it should not guilt users into finishing everything. [Sewing beginners discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/SewingForBeginners/comments/1u5yaxe/do_yall_ever_abandon_projects/)

Loose Ends is a nonprofit matching volunteers with textile work left unfinished because of death, illness, or disability. Its existence shows that physical projects can carry significant personal meaning. It is not evidence for our subscription demand, and Unpause should not imply an affiliation or borrow bereavement stories as a fabricated founder experience. [Loose Ends](https://looseends.org/)

## The wedge we can own

**Audience:** people with several hands-on hobbies who work in interrupted sessions. Start with sewing, embroidery, painting, and small creative builds. Do not begin with professional workshop management or safety-critical repair advice.

**Moment:** the user has 10–20 minutes, sees the half-finished project, and spends that time reconstructing what they were doing instead of doing it.

**Loop:**

1. **Pause:** photograph the work; record the stopping point, one next physical action, and the materials' location.
2. **Find a fit:** choose a 10, 25, or 45 minute window and see resumable projects with a reason they fit. Blocked projects should not be recommended as ready.
3. **Re-enter:** show one compact resume card with the actual saved context. Let the user verify it before starting.
4. **Make:** a quiet, optional session timer with no streak pressure.
5. **Hand off:** save what changed and the next small step, or celebrate completion.

**Demo:** a half-embroidered gift has sat in a blue pouch for three weeks. The maker has 12 minutes. Unpause recalls “finish the three small leaves; sage thread is in the front pocket,” starts the session, then records a new stopping point. The striking moment is continuity across a pause, not an AI chatbot generating a list.

**Voice:** calm, warm, concrete. “Ready when you are.” “A small step still counts.” Avoid shame labels, overdue alarms, productivity scores, clinical language, or unsupported claims about treating ADHD.

## Business model hypothesis

Offer **three active projects free**, with unlimited completed/archived projects and sessions. Reminders and data export/backup remain free. **Studio is a proposed US $19.99 lifetime purchase** that unlocks unlimited active projects, subject to actual store-localized pricing and configuration. This is a launch pricing hypothesis, not observed willingness to pay.

Recommend $19.99 over $24.99 at launch because the deliberately focused utility must earn its place beside free notes and free craft trackers. Ecru's advertised $24.99 founders price is a useful nearby reference, but its broader feature set and unconfirmed launch status make it an imperfect comparison. Test pricing with actual buyers; do not assert that a $5 difference increases conversion.

Never hide an existing user's memories behind a purchase requirement. Downgrade or entitlement problems must preserve read access and export. If an entitlement is missing, limits should affect adding beyond the free allowance. Restore purchases and clearly label Studio as a one-time purchase. Do not advertise cloud sync or any other feature until implemented and tested.

A lifetime purchase fits intermittent hobby use and avoids subscription guilt. The paywall should appear after a user has experienced a successful return or reaches the active-project allowance. RevenueCat coordinates entitlements and purchase restoration; the commercial reason to buy is keeping more projects ready to return to.

Keep the core loop local and deterministic. Structured user context delivers primary value without per-session inference costs. Optional future AI should reduce capture effort, turn a voice note into editable fields, rather than invent technical instructions. Lifetime Studio must not promise unlimited cloud media or perpetual costly services; backup/export can remain user-controlled. If recurring infrastructure is added later, fund and scope it separately.

Illustrative arithmetic, **not a forecast or recurring revenue**: 1,000 purchases at $19.99 produce $19,990 in gross billings before platform charges, taxes, refunds, infrastructure, and support. Future support obligations remain after the one-time payment. Do not call lifetime sales ARR or claim a break-even point without measured costs.

## Distribution and moat

The initial distribution hypothesis is makers showing the moment of returning to a real unfinished object: craft creators, local craft groups, and workshops can demonstrate the behavior in seconds. This is a proposed acquisition route; there are no secured partnerships or tested acquisition costs.

The MVP has no durable moat. Potential advantages to earn over time are a beloved ritual, reliable cross-device continuity, craft-specific handoff templates informed by consented user research, and a trusted personal archive. Avoid calling ordinary stored user data a moat. Exportability and privacy should increase trust rather than manufacture lock-in.

## Alternatives considered

| Opportunity | Appeal | Why not select it now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Memory tags for household objects | Easy physical demonstration; broader audience | Tapcord already directly serves that pattern. Broad inventory makes the proposition less focused and creates substantial capture work. |
| A service that transfers unfinished projects to another maker | Strong emotional story; a concrete completed outcome | Operational fulfillment, matching, shipping, trust, and marketplace liquidity exceed a software MVP. Loose Ends already serves an important nonprofit segment. |

These are qualitative comparisons, not a comprehensive market scan. Neither offers enough demonstrated advantage to justify abandoning Unpause's achievable end-to-end loop.

## Validation needed after the build

Recruit actual hobbyists with at least two paused projects. Observe them use their own project, not seeded content. Ask what they last forgot, how they currently handle it, and what they already pay for. Do not ask only whether they like the idea.

Measure first handoff completion, later return to the saved project, whether users find the resume card sufficient, and whether they save another handoff after making progress. Also measure capture time and whether materials-location fields are actually useful. A successful demo, download count, or fabricated testimonials are not substitutes for these observations.

Test the strongest competitor and a simple Notes template against Unpause. If users get equal value from Notes, simplify capture and sharpen the return flow before adding features. Present all conversion/retention goals as targets until measured.

## Pitch language

**One sentence:** Unpause remembers the next tiny step in your unfinished hobby, so a little free time becomes making time.

**Opening story, explicitly illustrative:** “You open the drawer. There is the embroidery you loved three weeks ago. You have twelve minutes, but you cannot remember the thread, the stitch, or what you were going to do next. So you close the drawer. Unpause is a way to leave that future version of you a handoff.”

**Product transition:** “Before you stop, save a photo, one next step, and where the materials live. When you have ten minutes, Unpause finds a project that fits and puts that context back in your hands.”

**Commercial close:** “Three active projects are free. Studio unlocks unlimited projects with one lifetime purchase. Our first validation question is simple: will people return to something they would otherwise leave in the drawer?”
